Moflin — Review

Artificial-intelligence pet with lifelike fur and evolving 'emotions'. Made by Casio.

Our review

Moflin is Casio's argument that a robot can feel like a living creature rather than a gadget. Beneath its soft, rabbit-like fur is a small platform that shifts and breathes, and its behaviour appears to evolve the more it is handled — Casio frames this as an emotional model that learns the texture of regular interaction. The result is a genuinely soothing, almost meditative object to keep nearby: it rewards attention with subtle movement and sound, and asks for nothing in return. That is also the honest trade-off. Moflin does not take photos, run apps, or hold conversations; it is a comfort object first and a device second, and the premium price reflects the materials and the brand more than any computing heft. For someone who wants a calm, beautiful companion that simply exists — a tactile antidote to a screen-heavy day — Moflin is one of the most polished, deliberately uneventful robots you can buy. It is best understood as a designed object for wellbeing, not a task-doer.

Pros
